# Task 2: Reflection

During the past ten days, the AI development that affected my thinking most was not a single product announcement but the speed at which multimodal models are becoming practical *agents* rather than passive chatbots. Systems can increasingly read an image, extract structured information, call tools, and complete a multi-step workflow with limited supervision. Working on the receipt-auditing homework made this concrete: a model could understand the receipt, but reliability still depended on decomposing the task, checking arithmetic independently, and keeping deterministic calculations outside the model. That changed my view of AI literacy. I no longer think the most valuable skill is simply knowing how to write a prompt; it is knowing how to design a system in which models, tools, validation, and human judgement each do the part they are best suited for.

This also changed how I think about my career plan. In fintech, I expect routine document processing, reconciliation, customer support, and compliance checks to become increasingly automated. Rather than avoiding that shift, I want to position myself between finance and AI system design: understanding the business process, identifying where probabilistic models are appropriate, and building controls that make the result auditable. The past ten days reinforced that domain knowledge will still matter, but it will be combined with the ability to evaluate, orchestrate, and govern AI agents. My plan is therefore to strengthen my skills in Python, LangChain-style orchestration, data validation, and AI risk management, while continuing to learn how financial institutions make decisions. The goal is not to compete with agents on routine execution, but to design and supervise the systems that use them responsibly.
